Research Interests

Weighted Nonparametric Maximum Likelihood Estimation for the Subdistribution of a Competing Risk

We developed a weighted nonparametric maximum likelihood estimator for the subdistribution of a competing risk, extending the Fine--Gray model to a broad class of semiparametric transformation models under right-censoring. The framework accommodates time-dependent covariate effects, and the estimators are shown to be consistent and asymptotically normal with robust sandwich variance estimation. The methodology was applied to a large bone marrow transplant dataset to assess relapse and mortality risks under different donor types and to derive personalized cumulative incidence predictions.

DOI: 10.1080/01621459.2017.1401540

Conditional Weighted NPMLE for the Subdistribution of a Competing Risk under Left-Truncation and Right-Censoring

We extended the competing risks regression framework to settings with left-truncation and right-censoring. To enable direct regression modeling of the cumulative incidence, we adapted the structure of a cure model and proposed a novel weighted-conditional likelihood function. The methodology was applied to data from the HVTN 503 preventive HIV vaccine efficacy trial in South Africa, where competing risks arise due to infection with different HIV-1 types, and only individuals who are 18 years or older and HIV-negative are eligible for enrollment. Our findings suggest that women in South Africa tend to be infected at earlier ages than men.

DOI: 10.1093/biomet/asaa034

Weighted NPMLE for the Marginal Mean Intensity of Recurrent Events with a Competing Terminal Event

We developed semiparametric regression models targeting the marginal mean of a recurrent event process subject to a competing terminal event. The approach is based on a novel weighted likelihood function and accommodates a large class of transformation models, including time-dependent covariate effects. The estimators are shown to be consistent and asymptotically normal, with a sandwich variance estimator. A novel simulation procedure directly targeting the marginal mean intensity is also proposed. The methodology is illustrated through application to data from the STATCOPE COPD trial.

arXiv:2605.25934

Personalized Risk Prediction after Stem Cell Transplantation

As a Mathematical Statistician at the NHLBI, I collaborated on a large multicenter cohort study within the Blood & Marrow Transplant Clinical Trials Network (BMT CTN 1704), investigating risks associated with allogeneic stem cell transplantation for high-risk hematologic malignancies in recipients aged 60 and older. Older adults are often excluded from transplantation despite their high disease burden, with individual fitness, comorbidities, and drug interactions. The team developed the CHARM model for personalized prediction of non-relapse mortality using multivariate competing risks regression and machine learning approaches including pseudovalue boosting.

DOI: 10.1182/bloodadvances.2025015793
DOI: 10.1182/bloodadvances.2025018340
